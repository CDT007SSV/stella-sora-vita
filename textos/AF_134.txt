a13411.c | A white-tailed titmouse landed in the snowy forest.\nHere there were no predators, no others of its kind. No one would challenge it for the seeds and frozen insects buried beneath the snow.\nBut the white-tail was not happy. It lifted its head toward the falling snow.\nPairs of shapes passed over the white treetops—\nBrown-feathers, flying free, side by side, together.\n\nThe white-tail could not understand the brown-feathers' way of life.\nWhite-tails only gather in pairs in order to hatch their eggs.\nFood was scarce, gathering in groups only meant less for each bird.\nLuckily, the brown-feathers didn't care for the forest. They were only passing through.\nThe white-tail tucked its head under its wing and enjoyed the tranquility of being alone.\n\nBut the sound of wings did not fade.\nCurious, the white-tail followed the sound and found two injured brown-feathers hiding in a thicket.\nTheir feathers were darker, their wings broader.\nBut on this white winter day, they were far too visible—\nunlike the white-tail, who could vanish from its predators' sight simply by keeping still in the snow.\nThe brown-feathers struggled to build a nest from dead branches that were covert enough for both of them, but with their wings broken, they couldn't reach far for materials.\nThe white-tail slipped away; after all, its path was different from theirs.\nStill, in the days that followed, whenever it passed by, it would drop a twig or two without thinking.\n\nThis year's blizzard was the fiercest the white-tail had ever seen.\nThough used to flying through wind and snow, at last it lost its way, its wings frostbitten.\nAs the white-tail closed its eyes, it thought of the two brown-feathers, and wondered if their nest could weather the storm.\nBy the time the white-tail opened its eyes again,\nit had its answer.\nThe white-tail found itself inside a nest—set up inside the thicket, big enough for three birds. The two brown-feathers' wounds had nearly healed. They had put the white-tail in the middle of the nest, shielding it from the icy storm.\nFrom the look of it, the nest had been built from the start with room for more than themselves.\n\nYears later, a human girl asked the forest birds: why do two kinds of birds with different habits spend the winter together in Philae?\nThe young white-tail and the brown-feathers told her this story.\nThe human girl tilted her head, lost in thought.
a13412.t | Where the Shooting Star Fell
a13412.c | The sun was setting, and Fuyuka sat on a swing in the park with a form in her hands.\nIt was the Employment Preference Form that her school had handed out.\nIn Philae, most students dreamed of joining United Harvest.\nGreenhouse work was considered the worst, loaders sometimes could get out of the office, and researchers were the most enviable—at least, that was the common view.\n\nFuyuka used to think the same way.\nShe thought of herself as perfectly ordinary: going to school like everybody else, growing up like everybody else, then finding a job and one day growing old.\nNormally she would have already filled in one of the popular choices and handed in the form.\nBut...\nThis year was different.\nCompared to the previous years, United Harvest's job openings had been slashed by half, and even the greenhouse posts that no one used to want were now high demand.\nFuyuka wasn't good at competing with others, nor did she want to.\nSo she didn't know what to write on the form.\n"If only I had someone to talk to..." Just as thought occurred to her, a classmate walked up to her.\n"Hey, sorry to keep you waiting."\nFuyuka fidgeted with her hands, not knowing how to respond to the warm greeting.\n"You're so slow! We might not get the best spot for watching the meteor shower."\nThere was someone behind Fuyuka—another classmate. The two girls walked off together, laughing as they headed toward the school.\nFuyuka quietly pulled back the hand she had half raised and sat back down on the swing.\n"Good thing I didn't say anything... Haha, hahaha."\nThe cold wind scattered leaves at her feet. Only the swing creaked in reply.\n\n"Are you lonely, miss~?"\nA raspy voice suddenly came from behind.\nAn old woman stood there with a crystal ball in her hands.\n"Hehehe... Do you want to alter your destiny?"\nFuyuka nodded quickly.\nBut the woman didn't go on. She only repeated herself.\n"Hehehe... do you want to alter your destiny? Destiny will give hints to those who truly want it."\nShe put emphasis on the words "truly want it."\n\nFuyuka understood; she pulled some money from her wallet to show that yes, she truly wanted it.\nThe old woman rubbed the crystal ball theatrically.\n"The direction where the meteors fall—that is your future."\n"Meteors?"\nFuyuka looked up. The shower streaked across the sky, falling in every direction.\n"So... Which direction is it really?"\nShe tried to make some more sense out of the fortune reading, but before she knew it, the old woman had already disappeared.\n"Hmm... What do meteors all have in common?"\nFuyuka stared at the sky and thought hard. The strange encounter with the old woman must have been a sign.\n"Ah, I see now."\nAs far as she could tell, all the meteors were falling beyond the city limits of Philae.\nFuyuka picked up her pen and put down "Trekker" on the form.\nA job beyond the city walls—in other words, a Trekker.
